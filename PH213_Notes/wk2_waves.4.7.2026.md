## Class 3
#### Waves
Both are sinusoidal and have similar properties.
##### Transverse
Oscillating up and down.
EMF, light.
##### Longitudinal
Moving back and forth.
Pressure changes relative to atmospheric pressure.


##### Transverse Harmonic Wave
$$y(x,t) = A\cos(kx-\omega t)$$
##### Period of Oscillation
$$P = \frac{2\pi}{\omega}$$
##### Wave Number
$$k = \frac{2\pi}{\lambda}$$
$\lambda =$ wavelength
##### Speed of Wave
$$V_{wave} = \frac{\lambda}{P} = \frac{\omega}{k} = f\lambda$$
##### Speed of a Wave on a String
$$v_{wave} = \sqrt{\frac{T}{\mu}}$$
$\mu \equiv$ Mass Density of String
$T\equiv$ Tension in the String

##### Wave Equation
$$y''(x) = \frac{1}{v^2}y''(t)$$
$v\equiv$ Speed of the Wave, depends only on the properties of the wave's medium

#### Speed of Sound
##### Bulk Modulus 
$$B = \frac{\Delta P}{\Delta V/V}$$

## Class 4
#### Waves and Superposition
##### Solution to Wave Equation
Negative $x$ direction
$$y(x,t) = f(x-vt)$$
Positive $x$ direction
$$y(x,t) = f(x+vt)$$
Note that the speed of the wave is already build in to the wave equation.
The speed of the wave is determined by the medium.

##### Superposition of Waves
$$y(x,t) = f_1(x = vt) + f_2(x + vt) $$
The sum of the waves appear to stand still
The waves look the same before and after

##### Standing Wave
$$L = N\frac{\lambda}{2}$$
$$f = \frac{v_{wave}}{\lambda}$$
##### Example: Guitar String
For a string that is 24" long,  vibrating at $247Hz$ if you place your finger n the 5th fret, the length changes to 18".

What is the new frequency?

$$v = f_1\lambda_1 = f_2\lambda_2$$
The string vibrates as a standing wave. Therefore, $\lambda = 2L$ .
$$f_2 = \frac{f_1\lambda_1}{\lambda_2} = \frac{f_1(2L_1)}{2L_2} = \frac{24^"}{18^"} \times 247Hz = 329Hz$$
