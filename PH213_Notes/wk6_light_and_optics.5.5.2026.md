#### Young's Double-Slit Interference
An interference pattern is obtained by the superposition of light from two slits.

#### Mathematics of Interference
In double slit diffraction, constructive interference occurs when $d\sin\theta = m\lambda$ (for $m=0,\pm1, \pm2, \pm3,....$), where $d$ is the distance between the slits, and $\theta$ is the angle relative to the incident direction, and $m$ is the order of interference.

Destructive interference occurs when  $d\sin\theta = \left(m + \frac{1}{2}\right)\lambda\text{ for } m=0, \pm1, \pm2, \pm3...$

#### Multiple Slip Interference
Interference from multiple slits ($N>2$) produces principal as well as secondary maxima.

As the number of slits is increased, the intensity of principal maxima increases and the width decreases.

#### Interference in Thin Films
When light reflects from a medium having an index of refraction greater than that of the medium in which it is traveling, a $180^\circ$ phase change (or a $\frac{\lambda}{2}$ shift) occurs.

Thin film interference occurs between light reflected from the top and bottom surfaces of a film. In addition, to the path length difference, there can be a phase change.

#### Michelson Interferometer
When the mirror in one arm of the interferometer moves a distance of $\frac{\lambda}{2}$ each fringe in the interference pattern moves to the position previously occupied by the adjacent fringe.
## Class 12
#### Interference and Diffraction Summary
Decreasing slit spacing increases interference, decreasing slit **width** increases diffraction.

**Minimum** means destructive (darkness interference fringes), **Maximum** means constructive (bright interference fringes)

**Coherent Waves** are in phase or have a definite phase relationship.

#### Key Equations
##### Destructive Interference for a Single Slit
$$a\sin\theta = m\lambda \text{ for } m=\pm1, \pm2, \pm3$$
##### Half Phase Angle
$$\beta = \frac{\phi}{2} = \frac{\pi a \sin\theta}{\lambda}$$
##### Field Amplitude in the Diffraction Pattern
$$E = N\Delta E_0\frac{\sin\beta}{\beta}$$
##### Intensity in the Diffraction Pattern
$$I = I_0\left(\frac{\sin\beta}{\beta}\right)^2$$
##### Rayleigh Criterion for Circular Aperatures
$$\theta = 1.22\frac{\lambda}{D}$$
##### Bragg Equation
$$m\lambda = 2d\sin\theta \text{ for } m=1,2,3...$$
#### Single Slit Diffraction
Diffraction can send a wave around the edges of an opening or other obstacle.

A single slit produces an interference pattern characterized by a broad central maximum with narrower and dimmer maxima to the sides.

#### Intensity in Single Slit Diffraction
The intensity pattern for diffraction due to a single slit can be calculated using phasors as 
$$I = I_0\left(\frac{\sin\beta}{\beta}\right)^2$$
Where $\beta = \frac{\phi}{2} = \frac{\pi a \sin\theta}{\lambda}$, $a$ is the slit width, $\lambda$ is the wavelength, and $\theta$ is the angle from the central peak.

#### Double Slit Diffraction
With real slits and finite widths, the effects of interference and diffraction operate simultaneously to form complicated intensity pattern.

Relative intensities of interference fringes within a diffraction pattern can be determined.

Missing orders occur when an interference max and diffraction min are located together.

#### Diffraction Gratings
Diffraction grating consists of a large number of evenly spaced parallel slits that produce an interference pattern similar to but sharper than that of a double slit.

Constructive interference occurs when $d\sin\theta = m\lambda$ for $m=0,\pm1, \pm2,\pm3...$ where $d$ is the distance between slits, $\theta$ is the angle relative to the incident direction, and $m$ is the order of interference.

#### Circular Apertures and Resolution
Diffraction limits resolution.

The Rayleigh criterion states that two images are just resolvable when the center of the diffraction pattern of one is directly over the first minimum of the diffraction pattern of the other.

#### X-Ray Diffraction
X-Rays are relatively short wavelength EM radiation and can exhibit wave characteristics such as interference when interacting with correspondingly small objects.

#### Holography
A technique based on wave interference to record and form 3-D images.

Lasers can produce sharp holographic images because of their monochromatic and coherent light with pronounced interference patterns.
