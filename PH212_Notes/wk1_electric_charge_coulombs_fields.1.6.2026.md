## Class 1
#### Electric Charge
Four fundamental forces:
1. Gravity
2. Electromagnetism
3. Strong 
4. Weak

Electromagnetism will be the main focus of this course.
Electromagnetic force is in a way responsible for electrical, magnetic, push and pull, tension, friction, air resistance, normal, and spring force.

Electric charge is the source of electromagnetic force. Think of it as a parallel to gravity.

While gravitational force is always attractive, electric charge has positive and negative properties giving attraction and repulsion.

Electric charge is an inherent physical property of certain subatomic particles. It is represented by the symbol: $q$

The SI unit of charge is the coulomb ($C$). Charge is conserved.

electrons, protons, and quarks have charge
neutrons, neutrinos, photons, higgs boson have no charge

The fundamental unit of electric charge is $e$
$$e = 1.602\times 10^{-19}C$$
All isolatable charged objects will have an integer amount of charge $e$. Quarks are the only real exception to this.

#### Conductors and Insulators
###### Conducting materials 
1. Have mobile electric charges that move freely. 
2. If excess charge is placed on a conductor, it spreads out evenly on the surface.

###### Insulators 
1. Have charges that are bound to atoms and molecules versus being mobile. 
2. Excess charges do not spread.
3. The electrons cannot flow through the material, but they can move away or toward a charge while remaining around the nucleus.

###### Direct Contact
When a charged object is brought near an object, the object becomes electrically polarized. Conductors and insulators can become polarized, the difference being the degree of polarization, or how far the charges are separated across the object. In conductors this will be more extreme.

###### Induction
Without direct contact with the charged object, forcing charge to move in opposition between two objects, then separating the objects "locking in" the difference in charges, thereby imparting a charge of the type of the originally charged object.
